Who forecasts Denmark's wind best?

Loading results.

Every morning before the 12:00 day-ahead gate, I forecast the next day's hourly wind power in Denmark's four wind zones, offshore and onshore in DK1 and DK2. Each forecast is committed publicly before its day begins. When the day is over, it is scored against Energinet's measured output.

J4catDK is my own power model. J for joint: one model instead of several averaged. 4 for its four weather models (ECMWF IFS, ECMWF AIFS, DMI HARMONIE, DWD ICON-EU), whose winds at 34 wind-farm sites go in side by side ("cat", concatenated), so it learns which model to trust where. DK for DKcast, my weather model's corrected winds at 22 Danish stations. A second stage learns how much offshore output gets switched off when prices are low. In its one test on 2026 data, frozen and published before it ran, its error was 5.56% of installed capacity, against 6.68% for Energinet's own forecast. How sure is the forecast?

Each forecast also carries an 80% band for total Danish wind: the range the real output should fall inside four hours out of five. The band comes from how far the four weather models disagree, and is centred on their average (the 4-model average in the table), not yet on J4catDK. When they agree, it is narrow; when they pull apart, as they do ahead of fronts, it widens.

Method

Each weather model's forecast at 34 points near Danish wind farms (100 m wind speed and direction, or 10 m where a model publishes no 100 m wind) goes into a gradient-boosted model per zone, which predicts output as a share of recent capacity. Every mapping was trained on that model's forecasts as they stood a day earlier, from Open-Meteo's archive of past runs, so the lead time matches a real day-ahead decision. The backtest tab shows 19 Jul to 18 Oct 2026, months none of the mappings saw in training. The live tab grows by one day every night.

Results are given on three standard scales. For the wind industry: mean absolute error as a % of installed capacity, per area, day-ahead. For meteorologists: skill score against ECMWF IFS, 1 minus my error over IFS's error on the same hours. For traders: imbalance cost, each hour's error settled at the difference between the day-ahead price and Energinet's imbalance price, per MWh produced. Accuracy, 100% minus error over output, is kept as a plain-language extra.
